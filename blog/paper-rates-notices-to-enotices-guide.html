From paper rates notices to e-notices: a guide for Australian councils

E-Notices Updated 11 August 2026 · 7 min read

Article

For generations, the arrival of a printed rates notice in the letterbox has been a familiar part of local government. Today, more Australian councils are giving ratepayers the option to receive that notice electronically instead. Moving from paper to e-notices can speed up delivery, reduce printing and postage, and make life easier for ratepayers and rates teams alike — but it works best when the transition is planned carefully. This guide explains what e-notices are, why councils are making the shift, and how to do it well.

Key takeaways

  • E-notices deliver a ratepayer's rates notice by email or an online portal instead of, or alongside, a printed copy.
  • Councils are moving online for faster delivery, lower print and postage costs, greater convenience, and reduced paper use.
  • A good transition keeps paper available for ratepayers who need it and relies on clear opt-in, verification, and communication.
  • Moving notices online is a natural moment to also offer flexible, secure online payment — the experience FlexiRates provides.

What are e-notices?

An e-notice is an electronic version of a rates notice delivered to the ratepayer digitally rather than as a printed letter. In practice this usually means the council emails the ratepayer a copy of their notice — often as a PDF or a secure link — or makes the notice available to view and download from an online account. The notice itself contains the same information as the paper version: the property details, the amount due, the due dates, and the ways a ratepayer can pay.

E-notices are typically opt-in. A ratepayer chooses to receive their notice electronically, provides an email address, and from then on their notices arrive in their inbox instead of the letterbox. Some councils offer a hybrid arrangement, where a ratepayer can receive both an electronic and a printed copy during a transition period. The key point is that an e-notice is about how the notice is delivered — the underlying rates notice remains the council's official document.

Why councils are moving rates notices online

The move to e-notices is driven by practical benefits for both the council and its community. The most commonly cited reasons are:

  • Faster delivery. An emailed notice arrives almost immediately, rather than spending days in the postal system. That gives ratepayers more time to plan and pay before the due date, and it removes the risk of a notice being delayed or lost in the mail.
  • Lower print and postage cost. Printing, enveloping, and posting notices to every property in a municipality is a significant, recurring expense. Each ratepayer who switches to electronic delivery reduces that cost, freeing budget for other services.
  • Convenience. A digital notice is easy to store, search, and find again. Ratepayers can keep a copy on their phone or computer, forward it to a partner or agent, and avoid the clutter of paper — without needing to phone the council for a reprint.
  • Sustainability. Reducing the volume of printed paper and physical mail lowers the environmental footprint of the rating cycle, which aligns with the sustainability commitments many councils have made to their communities.

These benefits are qualitative and will vary from council to council, but together they explain why e-notices have become a standard offering across Australian local government.

What changes for ratepayers

For ratepayers, the change is mostly a matter of where the notice arrives. Instead of watching the letterbox, they receive an email — often with a reminder near the due date. They can open the notice on any device, save it, and pay straight away rather than waiting for the post.

It is worth being clear with ratepayers about what does not change. The amount owed, the due dates, and the payment options are exactly the same as on a paper notice. Ratepayers who prefer paper can keep receiving it, and those who go digital can usually switch back at any time. Reassuring the community on these points helps build confidence in the new option.

What changes for the rates team

For the rates and revenue team, e-notices reduce some of the manual handling that surrounds the print-and-post cycle: fewer returned-to-sender letters, fewer reprint requests, and a smaller print run to manage each rating period. Electronic delivery also creates a clearer record of when a notice was sent.

At the same time, e-notices introduce new responsibilities. The team needs to keep email addresses accurate and up to date, handle bounced or undeliverable emails, and maintain the opt-in register that records each ratepayer's delivery preference. Good processes — and good software — make these tasks manageable rather than a burden.

Planning the transition

A smooth move to e-notices is less about technology and more about process and communication. A few steps make the difference:

  • Offer a clear opt-in and registration path. Give ratepayers a simple, well-signposted way to register for e-notices — online, over the phone, or on a form — and record their preference against the property.
  • Keep paper for those who need it. Not every ratepayer has reliable email access or wants to go digital. Retaining a paper option ensures no one is left behind and keeps the change genuinely optional.
  • Verify email addresses. Confirm the ratepayer's email before switching them across, and have a process for handling bounced messages so a notice is never quietly undelivered.
  • Communicate clearly and early. Explain the change on notices, on the council website, and through local channels. Tell ratepayers what to expect, how to opt in, and how to switch back — and reassure them that due dates and payment options are unchanged.

Running paper and electronic delivery side by side during the first cycle or two gives ratepayers time to adjust and lets the team refine its processes before scaling up.

E-notices and payment go together

Moving notices online is also a natural moment to look at how ratepayers pay. When a notice arrives by email, the easiest next step for the ratepayer is to pay online straight away — so the delivery method and the payment experience are closely linked. Next steps

Moving from paper rates notices to e-notices is a change worth making at a pace that suits your community. Offer the choice, keep paper for those who need it, verify email addresses, and communicate clearly. And because a notice is only useful once it leads to a payment, consider the payment experience alongside the delivery method.
